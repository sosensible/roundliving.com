# Stump Houses

Stump houses are elevated dwellings raised above the ground on timber, masonry, or concrete stumps. While many are rectilinear, the type belongs most naturally with vernacular climate-adapted building traditions, and circular or near-circular raised forms can be understood within that same logic.

## Why They Fit Here

Stump houses are best understood as a vernacular response to site and climate:

- They lift living spaces above damp ground and flood risk
- They improve airflow beneath the building in hot, humid climates
- They adapt well to uneven terrain and lightweight construction systems
- They reflect regional building knowledge more than industrial standardization

## Round-Building Relevance

In a round-building context, stump-house logic can support:

- Circular cabins lifted above sloping sites
- Raised eco-lodges and retreat huts in wet climates
- Vernacular-inspired round homes with shaded undercroft space

## Design Considerations

- Structural bracing is critical because elevation increases lateral movement risk
- Moisture, termite, and rot resistance matter at ground interfaces
- Stairs, ramps, and accessibility must be integrated early
