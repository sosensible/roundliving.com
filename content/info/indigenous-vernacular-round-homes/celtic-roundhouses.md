# Celtic Roundhouses

Celtic roundhouses were common dwellings in Iron Age and earlier European settlements. Built from timber frames with wattle-and-daub walls and thatched roofs, they provided robust shelter using materials available in surrounding landscapes.

## Defining Features

- Circular plans with central hearths
- Timber post rings supporting conical or domed roofs
- Wattle-and-daub wall systems for enclosure and insulation
- Strong relationship between house form and settlement layout

## Functional Advantages

- Circular geometry improves structural stability in wind
- Central hearth supports heat distribution and social focus
- Efficient enclosure with straightforward hand-built methods

## Why They Matter Today

Celtic roundhouses show how durable circular housing can emerge from modest tools and local resource cycles, offering insights for contemporary low-impact design.
