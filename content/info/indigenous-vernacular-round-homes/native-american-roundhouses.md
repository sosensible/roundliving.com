# Native American Roundhouses

Native American roundhouses are circular structures historically used by several tribes across North America. While design details vary by region and nation, these buildings often combine timber frameworks with earth, bark, thatch, or other local coverings.

## Defining Features

- Circular ground plans organized around a central interior space
- Timber post-and-beam or pole structures
- Openings and smoke vents integrated with fire use and climate needs
- Use as social, ceremonial, and sometimes residential spaces

## Spatial Logic

Roundhouse organization often supports communal gathering, shared ritual activity, and distributed seating around a center point, reinforcing social cohesion through architecture.

## Design Lessons Today

- Community-centered planning in circular rooms
- Climate adaptation through low-tech passive strategies
- Respect for material locality and cultural continuity
