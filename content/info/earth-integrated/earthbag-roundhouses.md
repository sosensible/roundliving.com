# Earthbag Roundhouses

Earthbag roundhouses are circular structures built with tubes or bags filled with moistened soil and stacked in courses. Barbed wire between layers creates friction and shear resistance, producing walls that are both durable and relatively simple to build.

## Defining Features

- Circular wall geometry formed by stacked earth-filled bags
- High compressive strength and good lateral stability when properly reinforced
- Very low-cost material inputs compared to many conventional wall systems
- Strong thermal-mass behavior for temperature buffering

## Typical Build Process

1. Prepare a stable foundation and drainage layer.
2. Fill bags with local soil mix and compact each course.
3. Add barbed wire between courses for interlock and tensile restraint.
4. Shape door and window openings with reinforced frames.
5. Apply plaster or lime render for weather protection.

## Why People Choose This Type

- Access to locally available material
- Lower dependence on industrial components
- High resilience in hot, dry, and seismic-prone regions
- Suitability for owner-build projects with training and careful supervision

## Considerations

- Moisture management and capillary breaks are critical
- Local code acceptance varies widely by jurisdiction
- Labor is significant even when material costs are low
- Detailing at roof-wall junctions strongly affects long-term durability
