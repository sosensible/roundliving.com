# Buckminster Fuller

R. Buckminster Fuller (1895-1983) was an American inventor, designer, and systems theorist who advocated doing "more with less." He is most widely known in architecture for popularizing the geodesic dome, but his broader contribution was a whole-systems way of thinking about design, resources, and human habitation.

## Key Contributions

- Popularized geodesic dome engineering for housing, institutions, and exhibition structures
- Advanced the concept of resource-efficient design at planetary scale
- Inspired generations of architects, engineers, and ecological thinkers

## Why He Matters to Round Building

Fuller demonstrated that curved and triangulated forms could produce extraordinary structural efficiency. His work helped establish domes as serious, modern building systems rather than historical curiosities.

## Lasting Influence

Today, Fuller's ideas appear in:

- Off-grid and experimental housing
- Lightweight disaster-response structures
- Sustainable design education and systems thinking
