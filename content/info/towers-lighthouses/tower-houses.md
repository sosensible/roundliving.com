# Tower Houses

Tower houses reinterpret historic fortified dwellings for modern use, combining compact footprints with layered vertical zoning. While they can be romantic in character, successful tower homes are rooted in practical circulation and service planning.

## Defining Features

- Cylindrical or many-sided vertical form
- Small site footprint with multiple stacked levels
- Thick-shell appearance or expressive structural exteriors
- Distinct room character by floor due to changing elevation and views

## Why People Choose Tower Living

- Efficient use of limited land area
- Privacy gradients by floor (public low, private high)
- Distinctive architectural identity
- Opportunity for dramatic stair design and light shafts

## Modern Adaptations

Contemporary tower houses often include:

- High-performance insulated envelopes
- Open upper-level living floors
- Roof terraces or lookout levels
- Hybrid structures (concrete core + timber or steel floor systems)

## Considerations

- Stairs and vertical movement shape daily life
- Mechanical routing requires careful floor-by-floor coordination
- Acoustic transfer between levels can be pronounced without detailing
- Accessibility planning is essential for long-term livability
