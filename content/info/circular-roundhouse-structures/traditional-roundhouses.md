# Traditional Roundhouses

Traditional roundhouses appear across many cultures because circular geometry is structurally efficient, material-economical, and well suited to communal life. While details vary by region, most rely on locally available resources and climate-tested construction methods.

## Defining Features

- Circular walls with conical or domed roofs
- Timber, thatch, reed, earth, or mixed-material assemblies
- Central hearth or social core in many traditions
- Strong adaptation to local weather patterns and material availability

## Cultural Contexts

Examples can be found in Celtic Europe, southern Africa, Indigenous North American traditions, and many Pacific and Asian vernacular systems.

## Why They Matter Today

- Demonstrate climate-smart passive design before modern mechanical systems
- Offer material-local and low-impact construction lessons
- Show how architecture can reinforce social and ritual space
