# Shell Structures

Shell structures use thin curved surfaces to carry loads through geometry rather than heavy material thickness. Inspired by seashells and other natural forms, they are common in high-end organic architecture where structure and sculpture are intentionally fused.

## Defining Features

- Thin-shell curved load-bearing surfaces
- Geometry-led structural behavior with minimal interior supports
- Strong visual identity rooted in natural forms
- Often paired with large-span, open interior programs

## Why They Matter

- Efficient load distribution across continuous curvature
- Potential for dramatic, column-free space
- Distinctive architectural language difficult to replicate with rectilinear systems

## Considerations

- Advanced engineering and modeling are usually required
- Fabrication tolerances and sequencing are critical
- Construction cost can be high due to custom detailing and formwork
