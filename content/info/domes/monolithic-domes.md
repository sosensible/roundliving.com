# Monolithic Domes

A monolithic dome is a thin-shell concrete structure formed as a single continuous unit — no seams, no joints, no separate panels. The construction process begins with an inflatable airform that acts as the mold, producing a remarkably strong and energy-efficient building once complete.

## How They're Built

1. **Foundation** — a concrete ring beam is poured at grade level
2. **Airform inflation** — a custom fabric balloon is attached to the ring beam and inflated to the full dome shape
3. **Polyurethane foam** — sprayed on the interior of the airform to create insulation and a base for the rebar
4. **Rebar installation** — steel reinforcing bars are pressed into the foam in a grid pattern
5. **Shotcrete** — concrete is pneumatically sprayed (shot) onto the interior surface, embedding the rebar and creating the structural shell

The fabric airform becomes a permanent exterior skin once the concrete cures.

## Structural Strength

Monolithic domes meet or exceed FEMA standards for near-absolute protection from:

- EF5 tornadoes
- Category 5 hurricanes
- Seismic events
- Fire (concrete does not burn)
- Blast overpressure

This makes them a popular choice in tornado-prone regions of the United States.

## Energy Performance

The combination of a continuous concrete thermal mass and thick interior foam insulation produces extremely stable interior temperatures. Many monolithic dome homes report heating and cooling costs 50–75% lower than a comparable conventional home.

## Living in a Monolithic Dome

- **Open floor plans** — no interior structural walls required
- **Curved interior surfaces** — require custom cabinetry and creative furniture layout
- **Excellent acoustics** for music or home theater
- **Low maintenance exterior** — the concrete shell requires almost no upkeep

## Considerations

- **Custom construction required** — specialist contractors are needed; the technique is not widely known
- **Cost** — typically more expensive per square foot upfront than stick-frame construction
- **Interior finishing** — curved walls remain a finishing challenge, as in all dome types
