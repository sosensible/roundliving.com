# Cocoons & Chrysalides

Cocoons and chrysalides are small protective envelopes formed around an organism during metamorphosis. Though they are not buildings in the human sense, they are powerful examples of efficient enclosure: minimal material, curved geometry, and precise environmental control.

## Defining Features

- Rounded or oval shell geometry
- Thin but highly specialized outer layer
- Strong relationship between interior stability and external exposure
- Built for protection during a vulnerable life stage

## Architectural Lessons

- A small enclosure can create a dramatically different interior environment
- Curved shells distribute stress efficiently even at tiny scales
- Protective wrappers can be light, temporary, and purpose-built

## Why They Matter Here

These forms are useful analogies for compact pods, emergency shelters, and minimal envelope design. They show how a carefully tuned shell can do a great deal with very little material.
