# Bird Nests

Bird nests range from simple scrapes to intricate woven chambers, but many of the most recognizable examples use circular or bowl-like form. That geometry helps hold eggs and young in a protected center while minimizing material and weight.

## Defining Features

- Circular, bowl, or pendant enclosure geometry
- Woven or layered local fibers, mud, leaves, or twigs
- Lightweight construction with surprising structural performance
- Interior shaped for thermal control and containment

## Architectural Lessons

- Flexible materials can create strong curved shells when arranged in tension and compression
- Form can emerge directly from function instead of ornamental intent
- Local materials can be assembled into durable, climate-responsive shelter systems

## Why They Matter Here

Bird nests demonstrate that circular shelter can be lightweight, adaptive, and materially efficient. They are especially relevant when thinking about woven structures, bio-based materials, and compact enclosure design.
