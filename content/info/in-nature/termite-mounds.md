# Termite Mounds

Termite mounds are among the most studied natural structures in biomimicry. Built from soil, saliva, and organic matter, they often take rounded or tapered forms that support structural stability while their internal tunnels regulate heat, air, and moisture.

## Defining Features

- Earthen outer shell with high thermal mass
- Complex interior passage network
- Passive airflow and humidity management
- Continuous maintenance by a colony rather than a single builder

## Architectural Lessons

- Ventilation can be designed as part of the structure rather than added later
- Thermal mass works best when paired with controlled air movement
- Durable natural materials can support sophisticated environmental performance

## Why They Matter Here

Termite mounds have directly influenced human architecture, especially buildings that pursue passive cooling and low-energy ventilation. They make the link between natural shelter and round-building design especially clear.
