# In Nature

Nature produces many shelter forms that echo the logic of round architecture: compact enclosures, layered skins, climate buffering, and efficient use of available material. This group looks at natural structures that can inform how people think about circular design.

## Structure Types

- [Beaver Lodges](/info/in-nature/beaver-lodges)
- [Cocoons & Chrysalides](/info/in-nature/cocoons-chrysalides)
- [Bird Nests](/info/in-nature/bird-nests)
- [Termite Mounds](/info/in-nature/termite-mounds)

## Why This Group Matters

- Shows how round or near-round form supports insulation, defense, and stability
- Connects architecture with biological shelter-making and environmental adaptation
- Offers precedents for passive climate control, layered envelopes, and material efficiency

Explore each type to compare how living organisms build protective round spaces and what design lessons can carry into human structures.
